What is Fit To Buy?

Buying your first home is one of the biggest decisions you'll make, and it's hard to know where to start. Fit To Buy is a free tool that shows what owning a home would really mean for your money, before you talk to a bank or fall in love with a listing.

It takes the pay, savings and spending you already have, and shows you what life would look like after buying in each suburb we cover: the loan repayment, council rates, water, power, insurance and stamp duty, all from official 2026-27 figures.

      First home buyer schemes

      Government help can make a big difference to how soon you can buy. Fit To Buy checks these for you in your results. Rules are for 2026-27 and can change, so check the official pages before you rely on them.

      5% Deposit Scheme

      Buy with a 5% deposit and no lenders mortgage insurance. In Adelaide the home must cost $900,000 or less. There's no income limit. After buying you generally can't keep more than 6 months of living costs and repayments in savings. Official details

      Help to Buy

      The government buys up to 40% of a new home or 30% of an existing one, so you borrow less, with as little as a 2% deposit. Income limits are $103,000 for one person and $165,000 for a couple, and the Adelaide price cap is $900,000. Places are limited. Official details

      First Home Owner Grant (SA)

      $15,000 towards a new home, an off-the-plan home or building a home. It doesn't apply to existing homes. RevenueSA

      Stamp duty relief (SA)

      First home buyers pay no stamp duty on new homes, off-the-plan homes or land to build on. On an existing home, full stamp duty applies. RevenueSA

      HomeStart Finance

      The SA Government's own lender, with loans for smaller deposits. Its Starter and Advantage loans have a household income limit of $110,000 a year after tax. homestart.com.au

      Buying your first home, step by step

      How buying a home usually goes in South Australia, and roughly how long each part takes. Every purchase is different, so use this as a guide.

      1. Work out your budgetA few minutes to a few days

        Check what you could afford and which schemes you may get. Start saving your payslips and bank statements, as lenders ask for them.

      2. Get pre-approvalAbout 1 to 2 weeks

        A lender or broker checks your income, spending and debts and says how much they're likely to lend. It usually lasts around 3 to 6 months. Ask about the 5% Deposit Scheme and First Home Owner Grant now, as they're applied for through the lender.

      3. Search and inspectWeeks to months

        Go to open inspections and ask the agent for the Form 1 (vendor's statement). It lists rates, easements and anything affecting the land. Book a building and pest inspection before you commit.

      4. Make an offer or bid at auctionA day to a few weeks

        For a private sale you make an offer, often subject to finance and a building inspection. At auction there's no cooling-off period and no conditions, so have your finance and inspections done first.

      5. Sign the contract and pay the depositSame week

        The deposit is usually held in the agent's trust account. If you have a small deposit, agree the amount with the agent before you sign.

      6. Cooling-off period2 clear business days

        In SA you can usually cancel by written notice until the end of the second clear business day after the Form 1 is served on you. It doesn't apply at auction, or if you've waived it with a solicitor's certificate. If you cancel in time, you get back any deposit over $100.

      7. Formal loan approvalAbout 1 to 3 weeks

        The lender values the property and gives final approval. Meet the finance date in your contract, or ask your conveyancer to request an extension.

      8. Conveyancing and settlementUsually 4 to 8 weeks after signing

        Your conveyancer handles the searches, the transfer and settlement. Stamp duty and the government transfer fee are paid at settlement. Arrange home insurance before settlement, as your lender will ask for it.

      9. Final inspection and keysSettlement day

        Check the home is as agreed a day or two before. Once settlement goes through, the agent hands over the keys.

      General information only, not legal or financial advice. Cooling-off rules: SA Law Handbook and the Land and Business (Sale and Conveyancing) Regulations 2025.

      Common questions

      Is it free?

      Yes, always, for buyers. There's no sign-up.

      Is my information safe?

      Your figures are worked out in your own browser. Bank statements are never uploaded. Nothing is sent to anyone unless you choose to send it.

      How accurate are the numbers?

      Prices come from official sales records, and costs from 2026-27 council, water, levy and energy rates. They're estimates for a typical home in each suburb, so a real property can cost more or less. Where a figure is less certain, we label it.

      Is this financial advice?

      No. Fit To Buy helps you plan. It isn't a bank and doesn't decide whether you'll get a loan. A broker or lender can give you a proper assessment.

      What does "Would a bank say yes?" mean?

      Banks test whether you could still afford the loan if interest rates rose 3%, and they use minimum living costs. We run a similar check, so you can see early whether a loan is likely to be a stretch.

      Why do you ask about my rent?

      Rent stops when you buy, and your loan repayment takes its place. Knowing your rent stops us counting both.

      Can I save and come back later?

      Yes. Press "Save for later" on any step and we'll email you a link that opens where you left off.

      What happens if I talk to a broker?

      Only if you ask, a local mortgage broker contacts you to talk through your loan options. You don't have to go ahead.
